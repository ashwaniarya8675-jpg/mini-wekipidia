SWAMI VIVEKANANDA

Swami Vivekananda (/ˈswɑːmi ˌvɪveɪˈkɑːnəndə/)[a] (12 January 1863 – 4 July 1902), born Narendranath Datta[b], was an Indian Hindu monk, philosopher, author, religious teacher, and the chief disciple of the Indian mystic Ramakrishna.[4][5] Vivekananda was a major figure in the introduction of Vedanta and Yoga to the Western world,[6][7][8] and is credited with raising interfaith awareness and elevating Hinduism to the status of a major world religion
